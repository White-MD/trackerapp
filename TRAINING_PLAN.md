# Olympiad Tracker v3 — Personalized 20-week curriculum

**Timeline:** 19 October 2026 – 7 March 2027 (provisional; verify actual competition dates).
**Primary priorities:** Physics (IPhO) and geometry-focused mathematics (IMO), astronomy deferred until after regional.
**Schedule:** Monday 17:15–19:45 (physics new learning); Tuesday 17:15–18:45 (flexible mentoring); Wednesday 14:15–17:00 physics and 17:45–20:00 maths; Thursday 19:00–20:00 light; Friday 16:15–18:15; Saturday 13:30–16:30; Sunday 10:30–12:30, 17:30–19:30, 20:00–20:30 review.
**Plan capacity:** 19 h 30 min per week; not a compulsory target. All times are local.

## Adaptive study rules

- Full day: complete the hard problem and reflect on the first useful idea.
- Interrupted day: 10 minutes active recall + 10–15 minutes on one micro-problem.
- School-test week: reduce hard sessions rather than borrowing from sleep.
- If independent solving collapses, spend 25–45 minutes generating at least 2 approaches before viewing a one-line hint.
- Whenever an error is discovered, revisit the problem after 2 and 7 days.
- Milestones are not gates: a regional training week can contain a carefully chosen international problem as stretch work.
- Monday emphasizes new material after school physics; Wednesday carries the biggest independent-solving workload.

### AC I — From differential equations to phasors

**Why this method?** The phase shift is not a memorized rule: voltage across a coil tracks the derivative of current, and voltage across a capacitor tracks the integral. For sinusoidal signals, differentiation is a 90° rotation.

**Prerequisites:** Kirchhoff laws, differentiation, complex numbers

#### Formal reasoning

Take i(t)=Re{I exp(iωt)}. Resistor: V_R=RI. Inductor: V_L=L di/dt gives V_L=iωLI. Capacitor: i=C dV_C/dt, so V_C=I/(iωC)=−iI/(ωC). Hence a series branch has Z=R+i(ωL−1/(ωC)). Because V=ZI, the magnitude ratio is |Z| and the phase is arg Z. The average input power is (1/2)Re(V I*) when I,V are peak phasors.

#### Method checklist

- Draw current as the reference and identify signed voltage phase shifts.
- Derive impedance using exp(iωt) instead of guessing phase relations.
- For multiple loops use complex Kirchhoff equations; transform to real and imaginary equations only at the end.
- Check low/high frequency limits and whether average power is dissipated solely by R.

#### Worked example

Worked example: a series RLC with R=3 Ω, X_L=8 Ω, X_C=4 Ω and applied voltage magnitude 10 V RMS has Z=3+4i Ω, |Z|=5 Ω, I_RMS=2 A. The current lags voltage by arctan(4/3). Average dissipated power is I_RMS²R=12 W. Check: as the net reactance approaches zero, current tends to 10/3 A.

#### Challenges

- For a series RLC, prove that the driving voltage and current are in phase precisely when ω²LC=1 (R>0). **Hint (read only if stuck):** Find Im(Z).
- Two series impedances Z1=R+iX and Z2=R−iX are placed in parallel. Find their equivalent impedance and explain why it is real for every X. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Use Z_eq=Z1 Z2/(Z1+Z2); treat the numerator as a difference of squares.

Further reading: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/cha12ac_circuits/, https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02sc-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-fall-2010/, https://www.ioc.ee/~kalda/ipho/

### AC II — Admittance, resonance, bridges

**Why this method?** Different circuit topologies suggest different algebra: impedances add in series; admittances add in parallel. Cleverly choosing the variable can turn a complex network into a symmetry argument.

**Prerequisites:** AC I, complex Kirchhoff laws

#### Formal reasoning

In parallel branches, complex currents sum: I_total=ΣV/Z_k=VΣY_k. Therefore Y_total=ΣY_k. For an ideal parallel LC, Y=iωC+1/(iωL)=i(ωC−1/(ωL)); it vanishes at ω=1/√(LC). This is large input impedance in the lossless mathematical model, not zero impedance.

#### Method checklist

- Convert parallel branches to admittance Y=1/Z.
- Use node potentials for loops with shared branches.
- Find resonance via zero imaginary part of net impedance or admittance; these conditions are topology-dependent.
- Optimize delivered power through a load using algebra or complex conjugation, noting conditions.

#### Worked example

Parallel R and ideal series LC: at ω²LC=1, the ideal series LC branch has Z=0, hence it short-circuits the terminals. That is opposite to an ideal parallel LC resonance. Topology is not a minor detail.

#### Challenges

- Find the frequency at which a resistor R in parallel with L and C draws current in phase with voltage. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Write Y=1/R+i(ωC−1/(ωL)).
- For (R+iX) and (R−iX) in parallel, compute total dissipated power for a fixed RMS applied voltage. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Each branch has the same voltage; find real Y.

Further reading: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/cha12ac_circuits/, https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02t-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2005/pages/lecture-notes/, https://www.ioc.ee/~kalda/ipho/

### AC III — Transients and driven oscillation

**Why this method?** One problem contains two timescales: the natural response set by initial conditions and the forced response set by the driver. Complex impedance solves the steady-state part only.

**Prerequisites:** AC I, second-order ODEs

#### Formal reasoning

For a series driven RLC, Lq″+Rq′+q/C=E0 cos(ωt). Characteristic roots r=[−R±√(R²−4L/C)]/(2L). For R²<4L/C, natural transients decay as exp(−Rt/(2L)) times sinusoids. Forced steady-state current is obtained with Z=R+i(ωL−1/(ωC)).

#### Method checklist

- Write one differential equation for charge q.
- Identify damping and natural frequency before inserting numbers.
- Solve homogeneous and particular pieces separately.
- Use initial conditions to fix the transient; check the transient decays for R>0.

#### Worked example

An initially uncharged ideal LC with initial current I0 obeys q″+ω0²q=0, ω0=1/√(LC). With q(0)=0 and q′(0)=I0, q(t)=(I0/ω0)sin(ω0t). Current is I0cos(ω0t), and total stored energy remains (1/2)LI0².

#### Challenges

- For a switched series RL circuit, derive i(t) and show the initial derivative satisfies Kirchhoff law. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Solve L di/dt+Ri=V with i(0)=0.
- Show why setting X_L=X_C in an RLC network does not suffice to describe switching transients. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Compare steady-state sinusoidal algebra with initial-condition ODEs.

Further reading: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/resources/cha12ac_circuits/, https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02t-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2005/pages/lecture-notes/

### Geometry I — Directed angles and cyclic configurations

**Why this method?** Most circle-geometry problems become manageable once you stop looking for lengths and look for repeated oriented angles. Mark equal angles, then search for triangles with two equal angles.

**Prerequisites:** angles in triangles, basic similarity

#### Formal reasoning

For distinct points A,B,C,D on a circle, directed ∠ABC=∠ADC modulo 180° because both are inscribed angles subtending AC. Conversely, if this equality holds and the points are noncollinear in a nondegenerate configuration, they lie on one circle.

#### Method checklist

- Translate concyclicity into equal directed angles modulo 180°.
- Look for a common chord when two angles subtend the same arc.
- Exploit similar triangles before applying heavy algebra.
- When a figure has many intersections, draw a second, cleaner diagram with only the necessary points.

#### Worked example

In triangle ABC let D be the foot of the altitude from A on BC and E the foot of the altitude from B on AC. Then ∠ADB=∠AEB=90°, so A,B,D,E lie on a circle whose diameter is AB (for nondegenerate configurations with four distinct points). The discovery is to create a circle from two right angles, rather than spot it visually.

#### Challenges

- In acute triangle ABC with feet D on BC and E on CA, prove A,B,D,E are concyclic when D is foot from A and E is foot from B. **Hint (read only if stuck):** At D and E look for 90° angles.
- Prove that opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180° and explain how directed angles remove case splits. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Apply the inscribed-angle theorem to complementary arcs.

Further reading: https://web.evanchen.cc/olympiad.html, https://web.evanchen.cc/geombook.html, https://www.imo-official.org/problems/?language=en

### Geometry II — Power of a point, radical axis, homothety

**Why this method?** When circles intersect many lines, products of directed lengths are often more stable than individual distances. The power of a point expresses this invariant.

**Prerequisites:** Geometry I, similarity

#### Formal reasoning

For a circle of center O and radius r, the signed power of P is PO²−r². If a line through P cuts the circle at A and B, then PA·PB=PO²−r² using directed lengths. Equal powers to two circles form a line (their radical axis) unless the circles are concentric.

#### Method checklist

- For a point P on two secants of the same circle, compare PA·PB and PC·PD.
- Subtract powers of two circles to obtain a line: their radical axis.
- Use signed lengths when the point lies inside a circle.
- For tangent circles, search for a homothety center before calculating coordinates.

#### Worked example

A point P has tangent PT to a circle and a secant PAB (P,A,B in that order). Right triangle OPT gives PT²=PO²−r². Secant theorem gives PA·PB=PO²−r²; hence PT²=PA·PB. Discovery: tangent-square identities are simply power-of-point identities.

#### Challenges

- Two circles intersect at A,B, and P lies on their common chord line AB. Show its powers are equal. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Both powers are PA·PB.
- Given two externally tangent circles, identify a homothety and explain how it maps corresponding parallel tangents. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Use the point of tangency as homothety center.

Further reading: https://web.evanchen.cc/olympiad.html, https://web.evanchen.cc/geombook.html

### Geometry III — Ceva, Menelaus and spiral similarity

**Why this method?** Cevian concurrency is fundamentally about conserving ratios as you move around a triangle; spiral similarity combines rotation with scaling.

**Prerequisites:** similarity, ratios

#### Formal reasoning

If D∈BC, E∈CA and F∈AB are interior and AD,BE,CF concur, then (BD/DC)(CE/EA)(AF/FB)=1. Conversely, under these conditions the product criterion implies concurrency (Ceva).

#### Method checklist

- For concurrency, express ratios using equal-altitude triangles.
- For collinearity, use directed Menelaus ratios.
- Look for pairs of triangles related by both a rotation and homothety.
- Check orientation and degeneracy before using ratio products.

#### Worked example

If D,E,F are the side midpoints, each ratio equals 1, so the three medians concur. More significantly, two equal-area arguments can derive the ratio product without assuming where the concurrency point lies.

#### Challenges

- Prove Ceva directly using area ratios for a point P inside a triangle. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Compare areas PBD, PDC and analogous triangles.
- Construct an example where ordinary, non-directed Menelaus produces a sign mistake. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Put one intersection on a side extension.

Further reading: https://web.evanchen.cc/olympiad.html, https://web.evanchen.cc/geombook.html

### Algebra repair — Transformations, inequalities, functional equations

**Why this method?** A strong algebraic solution begins by preserving structure: factorization, equality conditions, and substitutions often reveal which standard inequality or invariant can actually apply.

**Prerequisites:** real algebra, algebraic identities

#### Formal reasoning

For a,b>0, (√a−√b)²≥0 gives a+b≥2√(ab), equality iff a=b. Thus for positive x,y with xy fixed, x+y is minimized at equality. In Olympiad problems the important work is choosing the right x,y, not remembering AM–GM.

#### Method checklist

- Before AM–GM or Cauchy, identify signs and equality cases.
- Normalize homogeneous inequalities, e.g. a+b+c=1.
- For functional equations substitute 0, equal variables and negations.
- Check all domains and possible excluded values when dividing.

#### Worked example

For a,b,c>0 with abc=1, prove a+b+c≥3 by AM–GM: (a+b+c)/3≥(abc)^(1/3)=1. Equality is a=b=c=1. Alternative: apply (a+b+c)²≥3(ab+bc+ca) and combine with a bound for ab+bc+ca; notice this alternative adds work and is not always preferable.

#### Challenges

- For x,y,z>0 prove x²/(x+y)+y²/(y+z)+z²/(z+x)≥(x+y+z)/2. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Cauchy Engel form ∑a²/b ≥ (∑a)²/∑b.
- Solve f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y) over integers and explain why the same reasoning is insufficient over reals without regularity assumptions. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Prove f(n)=nf(1) via induction; think about pathological additive real functions.

Further reading: https://web.evanchen.cc/olympiad.html, https://www.imo-official.org/problems/?language=en

### Independent problem-solving — rebuilding discovery

**Why this method?** Recognizing a solution is a different skill from producing the first useful observation. Restore generation through retrieval, deliberate attempts, and very small hints.

**Prerequisites:** 

#### Formal reasoning

Use a four-column journal: statement feature → candidate method → why plausible → failure/success. Record the first useful idea and which cue exposed it. Count independent starts, not only completed problems.

#### Method checklist

- 5 min: restate the objective, draw and mark what is known.
- 10 min: produce at least two nontrivial attempts; test symmetry, dimensions, limits or special cases.
- 20–45 min: pursue the strongest attempt and record the first obstruction.
- Only then use a hint limited to one observation, close it, and try again.
- At 48 hours and 7 days: re-solve from a blank page.

#### Worked example

Example cue: “a quadrilateral with two opposite right angles” invites a cyclicity test because a pair of right angles subtend the same diameter. Do not jump straight to an equation: the geometric relation is the discovery.

#### Challenges

- Attempt one unfamiliar geometry problem for 25 minutes and write three candidate ideas even if unsolved. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Ask only which observation is useful, never the full proof.
- Re-derive one AC phasor equation from Kirchhoff law without notes. **Hint (read only if stuck):** Try complex exponentials and differentiate.

Further reading: https://web.evanchen.cc/olympiad.html, https://www.ioc.ee/~kalda/ipho/

**1. AC:** Given i=I0 cos(ωt), find inductor voltage and its phase relative to i.

*Hint:* Differentiate i; check the sign at t=0.

*Answer for checking:* v_L=−ωLI0 sin(ωt)=ωLI0 cos(ωt+π/2).

**2. AC:** For R=6 Ω and X_L−X_C=8 Ω, determine |Z| and phase tangent.

*Hint:* Treat Z as the legs of a right triangle.

*Answer for checking:* |Z|=10 Ω; tan φ=8/6=4/3.

*Hint:* Set Im(Z)=0.

*Answer for checking:* ω=1/√(LC) for L,C>0.

**4. AC:** Two impedances 2+3i and 2−3i Ω in series: calculate Z.

*Hint:* Add real and imaginary parts separately.

*Answer for checking:* Z=4 Ω, purely resistive.

**5. AC:** Two equal resistors R connected in parallel: derive admittance then equivalent resistance.

*Hint:* Sum reciprocals.

*Answer for checking:* Y=2/R, hence R_eq=R/2.

*Hint:* Apply F=−dU/dx.

*Answer for checking:* ω=√(k/m), provided k>0.

**7. Thermodynamics:** For an ideal gas expansion at constant temperature, is internal-energy change zero? State assumption.

*Hint:* Recall U depends only on T for an ideal gas.

*Answer for checking:* ΔU=0 (ideal-gas model, same final temperature).

**8. Dynamics:** A ball launched horizontally from height h: eliminate time to find range using speed v.

*Hint:* Vertical and horizontal motions share t.

*Answer for checking:* t=√(2h/g), range v√(2h/g), neglecting drag.

**9. Geometry:** Two angles subtend the same chord of a circle from the same arc. What relation follows?

*Hint:* Use the inscribed angle theorem.

*Answer for checking:* Equal directed inscribed angles modulo 180°.

*Hint:* Opposite angles are supplementary.

*Answer for checking:* 65°.

**11. Geometry:** A tangent from P has length 6; a secant intersects circle at distances 4 and x from P. Find x.

*Hint:* Use tangent–secant power.

*Answer for checking:* 6²=4x, so x=9.

**12. Geometry:** For triangle ABC, prove that its three medians are concurrent using a product of ratios.

*Hint:* Ceva with side midpoints.

*Answer for checking:* All three ratios are 1, so product is 1; Ceva gives concurrency.

**13. Geometry:** Find a simple cyclicity certificate for a quadrilateral with opposite angles 90° each.

*Hint:* Opposite angles sum to 180°.

*Answer for checking:* By converse cyclic-quadrilateral criterion, the four distinct points are concyclic.

*Hint:* Apply power PO²−r².

*Answer for checking:* √(13²−5²)=12.

**15. Algebra:** For positive a,b with ab=16, find minimum a+b and equality condition.

*Hint:* AM–GM.

*Answer for checking:* a+b≥8, attained at a=b=4.

**16. Algebra:** Is x²+y²≥2xy true for all real x,y? Show invariant.

*Hint:* Complete the square.

*Answer for checking:* (x−y)²≥0.

*Hint:* Reduce the base first.

*Answer for checking:* 7≡1 (mod 6), so remainder 1.

*Hint:* Color the board and count colors.
